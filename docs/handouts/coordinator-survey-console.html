Team Dispatch

HighTowers-Web — Wing planning aid for Low Level Route tower surveys

Audience: Wing coordinators, squadron leadership, and the pilots who fly the sortie files. The sidebar name and the page heading are both Team Dispatch.

What the sortie file is

There is no G1000 Parallel Track in these files. Every lane is its own set of user waypoints at the real offset coordinates. The G1000 navigates leg to leg between the loaded user waypoints, flown by hand or with the autopilot in GPS/NAV mode. HighTowers exports the .fpl. Before each lane, confirm the aircraft symbol is on the intended yellow track on both the ForeFlight moving map and the G1000 MFD map.

Inner and Outer

Inner is the side toward the inside of the route’s overall curve, as seen on the map. Outer is the other side. A nearly straight or S-shaped route has no clear inside. The app then uses Inner = left of the route as flown from the entry point, and it says so on the Sortie 1 side picker. Pilots go by the side shown on the picker. The labels are Inner (inside the route’s curve) and Outer (outside the route’s curve).

Waypoint names

Solo Flight Plans: point letters, the lane number, then I or O (A1I, A1O). Each sortie lists its side; fly the side shown. The file is already in flying order. A repeated place inserts a letter before the side letter (B1AI), which is not a lane number. Names stay within 6 characters.

Team Dispatch: point letters, the lane number, then I or O (B1O, XM2I). Think of the first one or two letters as the MTR point the waypoint is offset from. The 1, 2, or 3 is the offset lane of the serpentine route the G1000 flies to cover the corridor, counted outward from the centerline. The last letter says Inner or Outer. A rare repeat on the same lane adds a digit (B12I). Lane 1 is closest to the centerline. A team file has no published centerline fixes.

Before the SD card

Not a concern: per the Director of Operations, user waypoints and the flight plans that created them are erased after each mission. Confirm no leftover user waypoints before loading, because short names repeat between routes. After import, spot-check one waypoint against the plan.

Follow the G1000 Pilot's Guide for importing user flight plans from the SD card.

How dispatchers staff a route

Team Dispatch staffs one, two, or three teams. 1 team is one aircraft. Each sortie lists its side; fly the side shown. A later sortie is already in flying order; load it and fly it as listed. Do not invert the flight plan on the G1000. Each sortie is its own .fpl file. With two aircraft, each crew keeps one side on every sortie. With three aircraft, a later sortie may fly the other side of that crew's piece. Pilots resume a suspended search themselves. The screen is one page. Words in parentheses are the headings on the screen:

  1. Crews and aircraft (Teams & parameters).
  2. Route (Load the published route).
  3. Corridor and tracks (Scenario). Published widths are Left NM and Right NM. Lane lists are Left offsets and Right offsets. Those fields are not the Inner/Outer choice.
  4. Segments and refuel (Results).
  5. Sortie 1 side (the Inner / Outer radios).
  6. Results (download each sortie .fpl and email it to the Mission Pilot for that aircraft).

With two aircraft, each crew keeps the one side it picked on every sortie. With three aircraft, a later sortie may fly the other side of that crew's piece. This is not guaranteed separation; pilots still deconflict. Announce when you enter and exit each lane, giving your position by waypoint name (for example, entering B1O). The 1-team choice is one aircraft. Each sortie lists its side; fly the side shown. Pilots resume a suspended search themselves.

Map View

The map legend reads: “Map: ultramarine centerline with point letters, white edges, yellow sortie tracks, blue arrows.” The centerline color is #3F00FF. The centerline is the published route, labeled with the published point letters only.
